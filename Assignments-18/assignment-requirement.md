Introduction
This Week 18 Graded Mini Project asks you to apply responsible AI thinking in two ways: (1) analyse a real-world AI failure (bias, misuse, or hallucination) and explain what caused it and how it could have been prevented, and (2) propose practical safeguards for an AI agent in a domain you care about. This is a writing-based assessment focused on judgment, clarity, and realism—not coding.


Project Objective
Your objective is to demonstrate that you can:

explain how and why real AI systems fail, using evidence and clear reasoning,
connect failures to ethical implications (fairness, accountability, transparency, safety),
propose realistic preventive actions and governance controls,
and design safeguards that balance autonomy with oversight in a domain-specific way.

Part 1: Analyse a Real-World AI Failure (Bias, Misuse, or Hallucination)
Objective: Evaluate a real-world case where an AI failure led to bias, misuse, or hallucination, and analyse the root causes and preventive measures.

Instructions:

Choose one real-world AI failure. You may use examples such as COMPAS, the Amazon hiring tool, Tay chatbot, or a widely reported generative AI hallucination incident.
Use credible sources (e.g., investigative reports, official statements, reputable journalism, academic or policy sources). Include links or citations in your response.
Write 300–400 words and cover the five dimensions below: Context, Failure Description, Impact, Root Cause, and Preventive Actions.
Expectations for a high-scoring response (what the evaluator will look for):

Specifics over slogans: identify what the system did, where it was used, and what the failure looked like in practice (not just “it was biased” / “it hallucinated”).
Mechanism and root cause: explain how the failure emerged (data issues, objective/metrics, deployment context, lack of oversight, incentives, poor testing, unclear accountability).
Impact and ethics: describe who was harmed (users, communities, organisations) and why the outcome matters ethically (fairness, transparency, safety, trust).
Preventive actions: propose concrete mitigations (audits, monitoring, human review gates, documentation, governance sign-offs, guardrails, rollback plans), not vague “be careful” advice.
 

Part 2: Propose Safeguards for an Agent in Your Domain
Objective: Apply responsible AI principles by designing safety and control mechanisms for an AI agent within your professional or academic domain.

Instructions:

Choose a domain you work in or know well (e.g., education, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, retail, etc.).
Define a specific use case for an AI agent (e.g., teaching assistant, diagnostic support chatbot, financial guidance assistant, customer support agent).
Design three key safeguards under these categories: Data Privacy, Content Safety, and Operational Oversight.
Write 300–400 words explaining how your safeguards reduce harm and support responsible deployment.
Expectations for a high-scoring response (what the evaluator will look for):

Use case clarity: who uses the agent, what decisions/actions it can take, and what is out of scope.
Safeguards that can be implemented: describe what you would do in practice (e.g., PII masking, consent, access controls, moderation rules, escalation paths, audit logs, monitoring, periodic reviews).
Domain realism: align safeguards to the actual risks in your domain (what could go wrong, who could be harmed, how errors are detected and handled).
Coherence: structured writing that stays within the word limit and makes decisions easy to evaluate.
